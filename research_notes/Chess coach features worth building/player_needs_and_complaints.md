# Player needs and complaints: game review and improvement for players rated about 200-1500

**Method and evidence limits (read first)**
- Reddit (r/chessbeginners, r/chess, r/Chesscom, r/lichess) could not be searched or fetched: the search tool refuses reddit.com. Almost all forum evidence below comes from **chess.com forums and blogs**, plus a few Lichess forum titles, App Store pages and one review-aggregator page. Treat the picture as chess.com-centric and probably skewed toward players who post on forums.
- Most items are **single threads or anecdotes**. Where the same complaint shows up in many separate thread titles, that is noted as "many threads", which shows the complaint recurs but does not measure how common it is.
- Hard numbers found: one chess.com member survey (n=33,131, published Jan 2025), one aggregator analysis of 150 Google Play reviews (Jul 2026), App Store rating counts, and chess.com's published tier limits. **No survey was found that measures how many players review their games.**
- Some pages were read only through search-result summaries, not fetched directly. They are marked "(search summary)".

## 1. What do players at this level say they want from game review?

### Takeaway
The clearest wish that recurs across threads is **"tell me *why*"**: why a move was bad, and why the suggested move is better, in human terms and in lines a human could actually find. After that come **honest, non-patronizing verdicts**, **knowing what to work on next** (recurring patterns, which openings), and **proof of improvement** (rating goals dominate). The only hard numbers come from one chess.com survey of self-described improvers.

### Cited Findings
- **"Why" is the core need.** A chess.com OP (May 2022): Game Review "doesn't tell you why, and that's more important than just knowing the best move." Replies sent them to the Analysis tab, engine lines, or a stronger human — [chess.com forum: Is there a way to see why something is a good/bad move](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/is-there-a-way-to-see-why-something-is-a-good-bad-move)
- Same theme in other threads (search summary): the review "will just tell you if your move is a blunder/inaccuracy... and tell you what the best move is. But it doesn't tell you why," and its "Show Moves" line doesn't show "how you should had played" — [chess.com: Game Review terms page / forum](https://chess.com/forum/view/for-beginners/does-anyone-else-think-the-game-review-feature-is-kind-of-useless); [chess.com search results on coach explanations](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/why-does-the-coach-occasionally-just-not-explain-why-a-move-was-evaluated-the-way-it-was)
- **Lines a human can follow.** Users complain the engine's "correct" line is "like a 12 move long sequence" no two humans would see or play. Another says a move flagged as a missed pawn win actually set up winning a queen (search summary) — [chess.com: anybody find that the game review makes no sense sometimes](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/community/anybody-find-that-the-game-review-makes-no-sense-sometimes); [chess.com: Game review feature - is the coach always right?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/game-review-feature-is-the-coach-always-right)
- A 1600-1900 blogger (Jun 2023) asks: "Will a 1800 rated player really calculate 13 moves?" They argue missed deep wins and deep refutations overstate errors for club players — [chess.com blog: Why the Game Review is Not Very Helpful](https://chess.com/blog/DSTirana1/why-the-game-review-is-not-very-helpful)
- **Honest, not flattering, feedback.** A Diamond member (Jul 2025) says inflated "Brilliant/Best/Excellent" labels feel "condescending rather than constructive": "If I blunder, tell me... just honest, objective analysis." A reply agrees it feels "more patronizing" and claims chess.com profits when users feel good. Single thread, 2 substantive posts — [chess.com forum: Can We Get Honest Feedback from the Game Review Bot?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/can-we-get-honest-feedback-from-the-game-review-bot?lc=1)
- **What to study next.** After the 2024 redesign, a user complained the coach "does not give any advice on lessons to follow after the game" (search summary) — [chess.com: Why does coach not give me explanations…](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/why-does-coach-not-give-me-explanations-for-moves-in-game-review)
- Players stuck around 800 repeatedly ask what to study. Typical advice is to diagnose recurring mistakes, pick one area to go deep on, and do themed puzzles. Chess.com's own plateau post says "Figure out what's really holding you back" (many threads, e.g. "Hard-stuck in 800", "Can't get past 800 ELO", "stuck at the 700-900 range") — [chess.com: Plateau between 800 and 860](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/plateau-between-800-and-860); [chess.com announcement: Feeling Stuck?](https://www.chess.com/announcements/view/feeling-stuck-heres-how-to-break-through-that-chess-plateau); [chess.com: I'm hard stuck 800](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/im-hard-stuck-800-what-should-i-do)
- **Am I improving / rating goals (survey data).** Chess.com 2025 Improvement Challenge survey, n=33,131: **94% have a rating goal**, **56% want to improve openings**, **44% want better study habits**, 30% want to play more, 29% want to get better at puzzles. Improvers average **1153** at their preferred time control (site rapid average is **616**). Goals are 1500-1675, about 350-400 points above current. Experience: 25% under 1 year, 57% 1-5 years. After playing, the most popular practice methods are puzzles, game analysis and videos (no percentages given). Note that this sample is self-selected improvers, so it skews above the 200-1500 median — [chess.com: What A Chess Improver Looks Like](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-a-chess-improver-looks-like)
- **Mistake-pattern tracking across games.** Users who liked chess.com Insights valued per-opening breakdowns ("I don't really do well against the Caro Kann") and game search. One wanted "openings where a player had an advantage but failed to convert" (search summary; threads 2021-2024) — [chess.com: Thoughts on insights](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/thoughts-on-insights); [chess.com: Is Insights Worth Going Platinum?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/is-insights-worth-going-platinum)
- **Lichess users want mistakes turned into recurring training.** Forum requests ask for "Learn from your mistakes" to run across all games (sortable by time control) and to use Anki-style spaced repetition. One proposal says it "could offer coaching that currently only paid third-party software provides" (search summary; several threads) — [Lichess forum: create learn from your mistakes across all games](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/create-learn-from-your-mistakes-across-all-games); [Lichess forum: love LFYM, can it use all my games](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/love-learn-from-your-mistakes-can-it-be-implemented-to-use-all-my-games); [Lichess forum: personalized puzzles, turning game analysis into active growth](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/personalized-puzzles--turning-game-analysis-into-active-growth)

### Inferences
- A ranked "want" list consistent with the evidence: (1) plain-language why for the 1-3 critical moments, with short human-findable lines; (2) a recurring-pattern diagnosis across many games ("what's holding you back"); (3) turning one's own mistakes into retry or spaced-repetition drills; (4) progress tracking tied to a rating goal; (5) opening-specific feedback (56% want better openings).
- Honest labels matter to a vocal minority. Beginners may still like encouragement, so a tone that is "honest but kind" seems safer than either extreme.

### Gaps
- No Reddit evidence, so no upvote counts or frequencies from the largest communities.
- No survey splits wants by rating band (200-800 vs 800-1500).

## 2. Most common complaints about current review tools

### Takeaway
The chess.com Game Review complaints that recur across many threads are: (a) **estimated "game rating" is untrustworthy**, (b) **Brilliant/Great labels are inconsistent, rating-dependent or inflated**, (c) **Coach text is generic, sometimes wrong, and paywalled**, (d) **"best move" is engine-absolute and lines are too deep**, (e) **the free tier allows 1 full review per day**, and (f) **the 2024 redesign made the layout cluttered and best-move arrows spoil the answer**. Lichess complaints are mostly scope and bug requests rather than complaints about explanation quality. Aimchess evidence is thin: onboarding and direction.

### Cited Findings
**Estimated rating ("you played like X")**
- Many threads with titles like "Estimated game rating is a LIE AND DECEIT", "How is the estimated Elo… so inaccurate?", "Why chess.com game review elo estimater is RIGGED", "is chess.com's rating estimator accurate" — [search results list](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/game-review-estimated-game-rating-is-a-lie-and-deceit)
- Sept 2023 thread: a 400-rated OP was estimated at about 1100, while bots rated 1200 and 1300 were estimated at about 550 and 600. A reply: "Ignore the performance rating. It's some strange algorithm that's based on your own rating." Several replies told the OP not to buy Premium just for Game Review and to use Lichess instead. No staff reply — [chess.com forum: How is the estimated Elo… so inaccurate?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/how-is-the-estimated-elo-from-the-game-reviews-so-inaccurate)
- Users report that the same moves get different estimates depending on the players' listed ratings, and that a deliberate 15%-accuracy game was estimated near 2300 (search summary, single anecdotes) — [chess.com: Is chess.com's rating estimator accurate?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/is-chess-coms-rating-estimator-accurate); [chess.com: estimated rating bug or by design](https://www.chess.com/clubs/forum/view/estimated-rating-bug-or-by-design)

**Move labels (Brilliant / Great / Best)**
- Many threads: "Brilliant moves aren't all that brilliant" (7+ pages), "the new brilliant system is broken", "Wrongly given brilliant", "please stop marking this move brilliant", "whats brilliant about that i dont see it", "They missed my brilliancy" — [chess.com: Brilliant moves aren't all that brilliant](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/site-feedback/brilliant-moves-arent-all-that-brilliant)
- Chess.com's support article says classification thresholds vary with player rating. Users note Brilliant is more generous for lower-rated players (search summary) — [chess.com support: How are moves classified](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-and-etc)
- Duckfest (chess.com blogger, Oct 2024): the review "will pick the top engine move and call it best" even when several moves are about equal. The same move can be a blunder at 700 and only a mistake at 1600, and "Game Review is much harsher towards higher rated players" — [chess.com blog: Game Review Common Confusion](https://www.chess.com/blog/Duckfest/game-review-common-confusion)
- Blogger (Jun 2023): "a random move like h5 can be counted as brilliant too" — [chess.com blog: Why the Game Review is Not Very Helpful](https://chess.com/blog/DSTirana1/why-the-game-review-is-not-very-helpful)

**Coach explanations**
- Duckfest calls the plain-English Coach "entirely unreliable" and says he has never used it — [chess.com blog: Game Review Common Confusion](https://www.chess.com/blog/Duckfest/game-review-common-confusion)
- Generic text: the coach responds to a mistake with "That's an unfortunate error," "not at all explaining exactly why" (search summary) — [chess.com forum: Why does the coach occasionally just… not explain](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/why-does-the-coach-occasionally-just-not-explain-why-a-move-was-evaluated-the-way-it-was)
- Paywall confusion: "Coach Explanations are a Diamond only feature." Platinum users get them only on the first review of the day (search summary; several help threads) — [chess.com forum: Why does coach not give me explanations](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/why-does-coach-not-give-me-explanations-for-moves-in-game-review); [chess.com: Coach not explaining each move](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/coach-not-explaining-each-move-in-analysis)
- A forum regular: "Chess engines are nowhere near to understand and teach chess." The game review text is called unhelpful because labels reflect eval differences, not ideas — [chess.com forum: Is there a way to see why…](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/is-there-a-way-to-see-why-something-is-a-good-bad-move)

**Paywall and limits**
- Free members get **1 full Game Review per day**. Platinum gets unlimited reviews. Diamond gets unlimited reviews plus explanations. Gold lost unlimited reviews in the Sept 2023 plan change (search summary of chess.com support) — [chess.com support: Premium plans](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8562418-what-are-the-differences-between-membership-types); [chess.com support: September 1st plan changes](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8609584-september-1st-plan-changes)
- One user said a review that showed a Brilliant with no move attached "wasted their free daily review" (search summary, anecdote) — [chess.com forum: Wrongly given brilliant](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/wrongly-given-brilliant-in-game-review)
- Google Play aggregator (150 sampled reviews of the chess.com app, read 18 Jul 2026): **22% of negative reviews cite paywall restrictions**. The top complaint is bugs and lag (35%), then server issues 18%, ads 13%, UI updates 11%, cheaters 9%. Game review is not singled out. The store shows 4.69 stars from 3.3M ratings. The aggregator's method is unclear and it is automated, so treat it as weak evidence — [Glotier: Chess - Play and Learn Online review](https://glotier.com/games/chess-play-and-learn-online)
- Third-party "free unlimited Game Review" guides and "Stop Paying for Chess Analysis" posts exist, which signals demand to get around the limit — [chess.com blog: Stop Paying for Chess Analysis: 4 Best Free Tools in 2026](https://www.chess.com/blog/Alexander-PC/stop-paying-for-chess-analysis-the-4-best-free-tools-in-2026); [chessitup: unlimited chess.com game reviews free](https://chessitup.com/blog/unlimited-chess-com-game-reviews-free)

**2024 redesign / UX**
- May 2024 thread on the new Game Review: an extra click past a summary screen, features "HIDDEN in several different places", best-move arrows on by default ("the arrows just give you the answer"), the list of evaluation improvements removed, and reviews failing to load. Staff (Martin_Stahl) said there is no opt-out and they are "tracking feedback" — [chess.com forum: Chesscom's new Game review, what the heck happened?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/chesscoms-new-game-review-what-the-heck-happened)

**Lichess "Learn from your mistakes" and analysis**
- Mostly feature requests: run across all games, spaced repetition, move into the Learn menu. Also confusion over which errors get included (it includes some inaccuracies and not every mistake). Bugs: slow evaluation on mobile, and the best move not shown. Also praise ("amazing feature with a lot of potential") (search summary; several threads) — [Lichess forum: learn from your mistakes](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/learn-from-your-mistakes?page=8); [Lichess forum: mobile app LFYM very slow](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/mobile-app-learn-from-your-mistakes-very-slow-evaluation); [Lichess forum: LFYM is actually really useful](https://fi.lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/learn-from-your-mistakes-is-actually-really-useful)

**Aimchess / other tools**
- Aimchess iOS: **3.9 stars from 197 ratings**. Premium is $9.99/month or $71.99/year. Critical review (Sep 2023): no guidance on where to start, "like walking into a library, while expecting to walk into a classroom." Another (Jun 2023) accidentally taps "next" instead of retry. Praise: "one of the best apps for improving your chess" (2022). The free tier allows 15 lessons per day; premium unlocks the stats tools — [App Store: Aimchess](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aimchess-learn-chess-online/id1524941307)
- Chess.com Insights (Diamond) is being retired and replaced by Stats / Advanced Stats. Forum users called parts of it useless, e.g. "W-D-L record against guys named Bob" and win rate by day of week or time of day, while praising opening breakdowns (threads 2021-2024) — [chess.com support: What is Insights](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708925-what-is-insights-on-chess-com); [chess.com: Thoughts on insights](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/thoughts-on-insights)

### Inferences
- The estimated rating and the Brilliant label are the most-complained-about *features* by thread count, but they are also what gets shared. They seem to drive engagement more than learning.
- Accurate, specific "why" explanations are paywalled at the top tier on chess.com. That makes them the most obvious gap for a free tool.
- Spoiler arrows and auto-shown best moves annoy some users, which supports a "guess first, then reveal" (retry) default.

### Gaps
- No quantified share of complaints per feature. Thread counts were not systematically tallied.
- No useful Lichess app-store review analysis was found. There was no Chessable app-store data, and no review data for Aimchess Android.
- Coach-explanation complaints since chess.com's later (2025-2026) coach updates were not checked.

## 3. Features actually used vs ignored

### Takeaway
There is no usage telemetry in public sources. Forum evidence suggests the **accuracy score** is checked often but is contested, and the **estimated rating** is widely told to "ignore". **Coach text** is ignored by some experienced users. **Insights-style stats** were judged mostly useless except for opening breakdowns. **Retry/"learn from mistakes"** is liked where it exists, and users want more of it. Puzzles and game analysis rank just behind playing in improvers' self-reported practice.

### Cited Findings
- Improvers' most popular methods after playing: puzzles, game analysis, videos, then books and Chessable courses (ranked, no percentages) — [chess.com: What A Chess Improver Looks Like](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-a-chess-improver-looks-like)
- Accuracy: one reply calls it "reasonable" and more consistent than the Elo estimate. Another calls it "just an invention of chess.com that measures nothing" — [chess.com: How is the estimated Elo… so inaccurate?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/how-is-the-estimated-elo-from-the-game-reviews-so-inaccurate)
- Coach text: "never used it" (Duckfest, Oct 2024) — [chess.com blog](https://www.chess.com/blog/Duckfest/game-review-common-confusion). Workarounds users recommend instead: the Analysis tab with engine lines and eval, or "Show Lines" — [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/is-there-a-way-to-see-why-something-is-a-good-bad-move)
- The list of evaluation improvements (biggest eval swings) was valued: its removal in 2024 was a top complaint, because it helped find weak areas — [chess.com: new Game review thread](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/chesscoms-new-game-review-what-the-heck-happened)
- Insights: opening breakdowns and game search were valued. Day-of-week and time-of-day stats were seen as low value. The feature is now retired — [chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708925-what-is-insights-on-chess-com); [chess.com forum: Thoughts on insights](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/thoughts-on-insights)
- Self-analysis advocates (coaches): analyze without an engine first, note what you were thinking, then check with the engine. Keep a "lessons for next time" log (recommends Heisman's *The Improving Annotator*) — [GM Roeland Pruijssers, chess.com](https://www.chess.com/blog/GMRoelandPruijssers/how-to-analyze-your-own-chess-games-correctly); [Exeter Chess Club handout (PDF)](https://exeterchessclub.org.uk/sites/exeterchessclub.org.uk/files/AnalyseYourGames.pdf); [chess.com: How To Analyze Your Chess Games](https://www.chess.com/article/view/how-to-analyze-your-chess-games)
- GM Pruijssers says many players depend on automatically generated reviews (search summary) — [chess.com blog](https://www.chess.com/blog/GMRoelandPruijssers)

### Inferences
- Features that summarize *the few biggest swings* and *patterns across games* appear more valued than per-move labels across all 40 moves.
- The eval graph and opening explorer were barely discussed in the sources found. That is absence of evidence, not evidence of non-use.

### Gaps
- No usage data (click-through or retention) for accuracy, eval graph, opening explorer, retry, or puzzles from any platform.
- No source on the eval graph or opening explorer specifically for beginners.

## 4. Do players actually review their games? What stops them?

### Takeaway
**No survey or statistic was found on what share of players review their games.** Chess.com's 1-per-day limit plus heavy forum traffic show reviewing is common on chess.com. "Game analysis" ranks among improvers' top methods. The barriers seen in the sources are: engine output without explanations, lines too deep for humans, confusing or inconsistent labels, paywall limits, and UX friction.

### Cited Findings
- No figure is published for daily Game Review volume. Chess.com sees 15-20M games per day (context only) — [chess.com: Chess Stats](https://www.chess.com/terms/chess-stats)
- Game analysis is among improvers' top-ranked practice methods, but no percentage is given — [chess.com: What A Chess Improver Looks Like](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-a-chess-improver-looks-like)
- Barrier, engine lines: a 12-move engine sequence "two humans [wouldn't] have seen" (search summary) — [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/game-review-feature-is-the-coach-always-right). A bare engine score "may not mean much more than determining who is better" for beginners and intermediates (search summary of an arXiv paper, 2026) — [arXiv: Hallucinations on the Board](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04240)
- Barrier, confusion: "Is the game review broken or am I a fool?", "Game Review Confused", "anybody find that the game review makes no sense sometimes" (thread titles) — [chess.com: Is the game review broken or am I a fool?](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/general/is-the-game-review-broken-or-am-i-a-fool); [chess.com: Game Review Confused](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/game-review-confused)
- Barrier, time and effort: coach-style guides push short routines. These include a 10-minute review, 15 focused minutes over "an hour of passive line clicking", and stopping once you can explain the critical moment. Heisman (2005, old) estimated 20-40 minutes to play over an annotated game. Frequency advice varies: daily, 2-5 games per week, or one per week under 1000 USCF. These are opinions, not data — [lumichess](https://lumichess.com/blog/how-to-review-chess-games); [BeaconChess](https://beaconchess.com/guides/how-to-analyze-a-chess-game); [MyBoardGame](https://myboardgame-ny.com/blog/how-to-analyze-your-own-chess-games-and-actually-improve); [ChessGrader](https://chessgrader.com/blog/how-to-analyze-your-chess-games/)
- Barrier, the paywall: 1 free full review per day on chess.com — [chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8562418-what-are-the-differences-between-membership-types)
- 44% of improvers want to "improve study habits", which suggests consistency, not intent, is the bottleneck — [chess.com survey](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-a-chess-improver-looks-like)

### Inferences
- A free tool that is short (a few minutes per game), focused on the 1-3 moments that decided the game, and that explains in human terms directly removes the most-cited barriers.
- "Study habits" being a goal for 44% suggests streaks and light routines may help, though no source tested this.

### Gaps
- No measured review rate, completion rate, or drop-off point for any platform. This is the biggest evidence gap in this section.

## 5. What do they want from an "AI coach", and what do they distrust?

### Takeaway
Demand is clear from the many new "AI coach" products (Chessiro, Chessigma, AICoachess, ChessAIMaster, MyChessPlan, Take Take Take). They all promise the same things: plain-language why, the one recurring pattern costing rating, a personal plan, and drills from your own games. Distrust centers on **LLMs hallucinating pieces and tactics while sounding confident**, and chess.com's existing Coach being **generic or unreliable**. No independent user reviews of these AI coaches were found.

### Cited Findings
- What vendors pitch (a proxy for perceived demand, not evidence of satisfaction): "explains why a move was a mistake and what you should focus on" (Chessiro); "every blunder explained in plain language… personalized training plan" (AICoachess); "finds the one mistake pattern costing you rating… assigns the targeted drill" (ChessAIMaster); 20 positions from your games in a 30-day schedule (MyChessPlan, premium); 12 months of chess.com games analyzed for patterns (Chessigma) — [Chessiro](https://chessiro.com/blog/best-ai-chess-coach); [AICoachess](https://www.aicoachess.com/); [ChessAIMaster](https://www.chessaimaster.com/); [MyChessPlan](https://mychessplan.com/); [Chessigma](https://www.chessigma.com/supercoach)
- DecodeChess positions itself as explaining Stockfish's reasoning for players "up to 2000" (marketing) — [DecodeChess](https://decodechess.com/)
- Distrust, hallucination: Jennifer Shahade reported ChatGPT adding phantom pieces and Gemini confidently explaining a checkmate that wasn't one. She warns that models get more persuasive when wrong (search summary) — [Time, Apr 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/04/13/why-i-play-chess-against-chatgpt/); [Shahade note](https://substack.com/@jenshahade/note/c-243241750)
- Benchmarks: in ChessQA, GPT-5 with thinking reached 79.3%. Failure modes include board-state hallucination and wrong piece or square recognition (search summary) — [arXiv: ChessQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23948). A tool-augmented evaluation of LLM commentary catalogs claims about pieces on the wrong squares and false before-and-after comparisons (search summary) — [arXiv: Hallucinations on the Board](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04240). LLM explanations often rely on surface observations rather than transferable concepts (search summary) — [arXiv: Explaining Chess and Go in Natural Language](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23166)
- Distrust of the existing chess.com Coach: "entirely unreliable" — [Duckfest](https://www.chess.com/blog/Duckfest/game-review-common-confusion). Also generic phrasing ("That's an unfortunate error") — [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/why-does-the-coach-occasionally-just-not-explain-why-a-move-was-evaluated-the-way-it-was)
- Some players reject engine or automatic "why" entirely: "You should be trying to figure out why, not the engine" — [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/game-analysis/is-there-a-way-to-see-why-something-is-a-good-bad-move)
- A controlled study compared no explanation vs placebo vs actionable explanations for learners on forks and pins (search summary; results not extracted) — [arXiv 2511.03730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03730)

### Inferences
- Trust likely depends on explanations grounded in verifiable engine facts (the concrete line, the piece that hangs, the fork) rather than free-form LLM prose. Showing the line on the board lets users check the claim.
- The AI-coach market is crowded with near-identical promises, so differentiation would come from accuracy, being free, and focus, not from the "AI coach" label itself.

### Gaps
- No independent user reviews or ratings of the AI-coach products above. No Reddit sentiment on "AI chess coach" was available.
- No survey on how much players trust LLM chess explanations.

## 6. Surveys and app-store analyses with numbers

### Takeaway
Only a few quantified sources exist: the chess.com improver survey (n=33,131), one automated Google Play review analysis (n=150), App Store rating counts, and chess.com tier limits. None measure satisfaction with game review specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Chess.com 2025 Improvement Challenge survey: n=33,131 (published Jan 16, 2025). Average rating 1153. 94% have a rating goal, 56% openings, 44% study habits, 30% play more, 29% puzzles. Goals are +350-400 points — [chess.com](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-a-chess-improver-looks-like)
- Chess.com Android app (Glotier, 150 reviews, Jul 2026). Negatives (n=46): bugs and lag 35%, paywall 22%, servers 18%, ads 13%, UI updates 11%, cheaters 9%, streak loss 7%, chat harassment 4%. Positives (n=93): fun 28%, good for learning 24%, customization 12%, ad-free 10%. Weak method — [Glotier](https://glotier.com/games/chess-play-and-learn-online)
- Aimchess iOS: 3.9 stars, 197 ratings; $9.99/month or $71.99/year — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aimchess-learn-chess-online/id1524941307)
- Chess.com free tier: 1 Game Review per day. Coach explanations are full only on Diamond — [chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8562418-what-are-the-differences-between-membership-types); [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/why-does-coach-not-give-me-explanations-for-moves-in-game-review)

### Inferences
- Paywall frustration is measurable (22% of negative app reviews in one sample) and matches the forum advice to use Lichess for free analysis. A free review tool targets a real complaint.

### Gaps
- No Lichess app, Chessable, or Aimchess Android review analyses were found. No academic survey of novice review habits was found. Reddit is inaccessible to this toolset, so polls or megathreads there could not be checked.
